Propagation is the process by which something spreads or travels from one place to another. The exact meaning depends on the context:

In physics/waves, propagation means a wave traveling through a medium or space (like sound waves or light waves).
In plants/biology, propagation means reproducing or multiplying organisms, such as growing new plants from seeds or cuttings.
In computing/networking, propagation can refer to how signals or data travel through a network.
In electronics, propagation delay is the time it takes for a signal to move through a circuit.
In social contexts, propagation means the spread of ideas, information, or rumors.

Line-of-Sight (Space) Waves: Used by higher frequencies (like VHF/UHF, Wi-Fi, and cell phones). Waves travel in a straight line and are limited by the physical horizon and obstacles.
Sky Waves: Used by High Frequency (HF) shortwave signals. These waves bounce off the Earth's ionosphere, allowing them to travel around the curve of the Earth and achieve long-distance, global communication.
Ground Waves: Lower frequency waves (like AM radio) physically hug the curvature of the Earth's surface, making them ideal for reliable, medium-range local broadcasting
